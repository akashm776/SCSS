# 8. Conclusion

[Previous](limitations.md) · [Contents](../../README.md#contents) · [Next](references.md)

The completed studies show that a synthetic-negative intervention's reporting-loss utility can depend on the tested learner state. History, optimizer-reset, schedule, difficulty, and pressure controls narrow several explanations. The common-population source study finds a consistent early synthesis advantage over its specified real-negative controls, but no reliable source-side curriculum and no aggregate late loss benefit over native.

These results do not establish a general training improvement: early loss gains coexist with worse aggregate retrieval. Source labels also do not determine the geometry of the generated candidate, motivating a more direct placement comparison.

The placement study has passed staged numerical, gradient, and calibration checks. Its full-population audit and utility experiment remain pending. Future conclusions must depend on those results, new replication, and evaluation beyond the reused development setting.
